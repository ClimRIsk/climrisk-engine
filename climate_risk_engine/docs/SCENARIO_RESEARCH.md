# Scenario research: which perils drive loss, by sector and region

Compiled October 2026 from public insurer, reinsurer, loss-adjuster and trade sources. Source quality varies and is stated. Figures from different datasets are not comparable and are not merged. Where a search found nothing, that is recorded as a gap, not filled from memory.

## Cross-cutting findings

- Secondary perils are now most of insured nat-cat loss. Swiss Re sigma 1/2026: 92 % of USD 107 billion in 2025 (wildfire, severe convective storm and flood alone 88 %). Munich Re: about USD 98 billion of USD 108 billion non-peak. In North America 99.9 % (Swiss Re). Severe convective storm USD 42 billion in the US (Munich Re), 46 billion in North America (Swiss Re); Palisades and Eaton fires about USD 40 billion insured.
- Protection gap in Asia: Swiss Re puts 2025 insured losses at USD 5.2 billion against USD 65 billion economic, 8 % insured. Aon: Asia-Pacific economic loss USD 76 billion, about 10 % insured. Factories, ports and logistics hubs concentrate value in typhoon, surge and flood zones. FM reports insurers in Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore shortening cover or excluding perils for factories.
- Contingent business interruption dominates manufacturing events. Thailand 2011: insured loss USD 10 to 15 billion (estimates differ), almost all manufacturing and supply chain; 40 to 45 % of world hard-disk output; BI roughly 15 to 20 % of claims because take-up was low. Tohoku 2011: RMS said contingent BI was the largest model uncertainty; Swiss Re said it was not explicitly captured in models.
- Open weather and hazard data are smooth. Reanalysis grids underestimate convective downpours, hail and gusts. Several results below are failures of exactly this kind.

## Sector by sector

| Sector | What drives loss | Evidence | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offshore wind | Subsea cables first; turbine components second; foundations; lightning; installation | Allianz Commercial 2014-2020: cables 53 % of claims by value, turbine failure 20 %. GCube: cabling 55 % and foundations 35 % over 12 months. DNV: a loss adjuster puts cable failures near 80 % of financial losses. DNV statement relayed by press: lightning the most common claim; a loss adjuster: 60 % of operational blade losses | Allianz, GCube: insurer data. DNV figure: a loss adjuster's, different basis. Lightning: press relay and a vendor source |
| Offshore wind, cold | Salt lowers the freezing point of spray so blade icing is rarer offshore; sea ice affects foundations and vessel access in the Baltic and in cold winters in shallow seas; freeze-thaw degrades concrete and grout in the tidal and splash zone slowly | Strathclyde 2025 (sea ice and O&M, Nova Scotia case: inaccessible days 53 without ice class vs 18 with IB); salinity icing lab study (thickness 17.2 mm to 6.0 mm as salinity rises to 35 per mille); freeze-induced foundation degradation simulation | Academic; no public offshore claims for icing or freeze-thaw found; no Danish or Wadden Sea ice statistics found |
| Solar | Hail first | Midway, Texas 2019: 58 % of 685,000 modules, claim about USD 70 to 75 million. DNV: Texas PV hail losses above USD 600 million since 2018. Hail of 3 cm or more damages modules; glass thickness matters (2.8 mm lost 21.8 % of power at 45 mm, 4 mm lost 0.8 %). GCube expected over USD 300 million in 2019 Texas hail claims | Trade press and testing studies; Europe: no solid solar-specific loss dataset found |
| Refining, petrochemical | Hurricane, river flood, surge, **freeze**, and business interruption | AIChE with FM Global: coastal chemical plants exposed to hurricanes and floods; losses rising for 30 years. Uri 2021: Dow downstream insured USD 600 to 650 million; Marsh: site damage far below its 189 million 100-largest-losses threshold per site but large cumulative BI; 2.0 to 5.2 million b/d refining capacity offline | Insurance Insider; Marsh; trade press. No total insured Uri loss found |
| Power grid | Ice storms, wind, freeze, wildfire | Eastern Canada ice storm 1998: insured about CAD 1.5 to 2.2 billion by source and dollar basis, nearly a million without power. Uri: about two thirds of ERCOT generation offline | IBC via press |
| Real estate, Canada | Wildfire, hail | Fort McMurray 2016 CAD 3.58 billion insured. Calgary hail 13 June 2020 almost CAD 1.2 billion (CatIQ), most of it personal property and vehicles. Six of Canada's ten costliest disasters hit Alberta | IBC, CatIQ via press |
| Real estate, Europe | Windstorm, hail, flood | PERILS original insured property loss: Lothar EUR 5.77 billion, Kyrill EUR 3.65 billion, Friederike EUR 1.47 billion. Northern Italy hail July 2023 about EUR 6 billion, all property. Ahr valley flood July 2021: 134 deaths in the valley (DKKV), German insured about EUR 8.2 billion (insurers' associations via Verisk), discharge far above statistical 100-year values. Southern Germany floods 2024 PERILS EUR 1.56 billion | PERILS, DKKV, NHESS: good. Hail figure is a review's summary |
| Gulf industry and property | Heat, **flash flood**, dust, water | UAE April 2024: 254.8 mm in 24 h at Khatam Al Shaklah, 142 mm in a day in Dubai (heaviest since 1949); insured USD 0.65 to 0.85 billion early (Guy Carpenter) rising to 1.5 to 2.8 billion (S&P and other estimates); about 30,000 to 50,000 vehicles | Guy Carpenter, S&P: reinsurer and rating-agency; dust and industrial-asset exposure: nothing found |
| South and Southeast Asia manufacturing | River flood, typhoon, heat-hours lost, contingent BI | Thailand 2011 (above); Aon: working hours lost to heat in Asia more than doubled in three decades, most in construction, agriculture and manufacturing; late-2025 cyclones about USD 20 billion | Aon, FM, Swiss Re |
| Mining | Flash flood and tailings, landslide, water, thaw | Thermosyphons keep arsenic frozen at Canadian mines; up to half of Arctic infrastructure at high thaw risk by 2050 (review via Reuters), 9,500 km of Arctic pipelines (satellite estimate) | Review and press; no mining insured-loss data found |
| Data centres | Heat and water for cooling and suppression, wildfire, power | FM Resilience Index 2026: water stress and fire risk linked, critical for data centres | FM press |
| Ports | Surge, typhoon, sea level, earthquake | Swiss Re Asia 2025: protection gap linked to ports and logistics hubs in typhoon and surge zones | Swiss Re |
| Hydropower, agriculture, pipelines | Drought, glacier lake outburst, hail, ground movement | No quantitative public loss sources found in these searches. The engine's lists for them are marked as judgement | Gap |

## What the searches did not find (do not fill from memory)
